English details follow. Research date: **2026-10-07**. The fresh rail architecture supersedes the earlier mobile/humanoid designs in the copied inputs. The market role evaluates buyable hardware, installation assumptions and cost. It does not inherit their old torque ceilings or feasibility results.

The architecture decision is a back-wall beam carrying two or three independently powered carriages, with an arm mounted sideways, at an engineered angle, or upright on a carriage shelf as its manual allows. No shortlisted arm is marked fully verified on all binding requirements. A current/torque-based soft stop is not a power-off brake. Adding safety sensors cannot cure an unbraked gravity axis.

A multi-carriage rail needs independent actuators, not just several bearing blocks. The selected budgeting concept uses a shared structural beam, two parallel guideways, a fixed rack and one geared servo/encoder/drive/spring brake per carriage. Each carriage has four guide blocks and an angled or shelf adapter. Travel is horizontal; the carriage position freezes throughout each process stroke. Unused arms park in explicit collision-checked envelopes. Carriages cannot pass on a single lane: preserve their left-to-right order, assign wash/central-support/cutting zones, and resolve shared tool access with reach or a second lane. Do not assume three arms can all access every station simultaneously.

The **6,000 CNY/m** structural rail package and **15,000 CNY/complete driven carriage** in the cost model are engineering allowances. They include much more than the public raw-component comparisons. A supplier-validated price for a complete independent, braked, wall-mounted multi-arm track was not publicly available. The exact guide sizes, carriage spacing and beam supports must be selected against both moment and force; a “50kg payload” axis advertisement is insufficient.

A normally closed spring brake on each drive is mandatory even though the nominal rail is horizontal: it prevents unintended carriage drift and holds a parked arm after supply loss. Select a brake with a rated rack holding force based on gearing/efficiency and worst external/process/cable load; monitor commanded release and engagement. A [Mayr electrically released, spring-applied ROBA-linearstop](https://www.mayr.com/de/produkte/sicherheitsbremsen/industriebremsen/roba-linearstop-elektrisch~35072) is an available guide/rod-clamp approach, but its exact rod/bracket/capacity/price is not selected. A motor brake must not be credited as a dynamic emergency brake without its manufacturer's rating.

Use sloped, drainable stainless covers over rack/bearings, guide wipers, sealed glands and service panels opening away from food. Enclosed [Igus cable tubes](https://eapi.igus.cn/ecom/media/cn-igus-strapi-bucket/file/ECS_CAT_quick_delivery_catalog_CN_5_b140f9108e.pdf) are relevant to splash/grease protection. Cable bend radius, PE continuity, current capacity and full independent-carriage travel must be checked with real arm cables. The [China E4.42 example](https://www.igus.cn/product/series-E4-42-ESD?category=e41-esd) displays **774.57 CNY including tax for one link plus a connector set**, not a metre or closed tube; it is not extrapolated into a fictitious installed-chain price. Budget **3,000 CNY/arm** for enclosed carrier, rated flexible cables, glands and installation. Mount each chain in its own lane; a loose loop between arms is not an independent cable system. Covered machinery is not automatically safe for pressure washing or direct food contact.

For an L kitchen, prefer separate straight axes with a controlled handoff unless actual tasks demand traversal. A continuous curved MHD route requires bogie rotation, bend-compatible rack/drive geometry, end-to-end cable routing and a fresh arm/faucet/cabinet envelope evaluation. Neither a standard straight belt module nor a tight e-chain bend provides this automatically. A **30k–80k CNY extra allowance before contingency** is recorded for a custom corner, with no public matched quotation. It is excluded from the 2.4m straight-rail totals.

A front/ceiling rail is only a comparison: it can reach the counter front more directly, but adds overhead loads, cables and obstruction in the user's working aisle. A back-wall low or angled mount remains the chosen route. Arms hanging below upper cabinets can satisfy the preferred location **only when loads go into an independent structural frame/wall**, not cabinet particleboard, and their swept volumes permit cabinet use.

Anchor loads were calculated for each candidate at full nominal reach, with **3kg food + 2kg EOAT**, a 15kg carriage/adapter, 120mm wall standoff and the requested dynamic factor 1.5. The preliminary arm self-COM is assumed at R/2; a second conservative envelope places the entire arm mass at full reach. This is a bounding gravity calculation, not a detailed OEM mass distribution or structural approval:

`V = 1.5 g (m_arm + 5 + 15)`

`M_wall = 1.5 g [m_arm (0.12 + R/2) + 5 (0.12 + R) + 15 × 0.12]`

For a four-anchor bracket with two upper anchors and vertical row pitch h=0.25m, `N_upper = M/(2h)` and the initial equal-share shear is `V/4`. Horizontal row pitch is provisionally 0.20m. Cable drag, motor acceleration, tool reactions, torsion, bracket prying, substrate and rail-support load distribution are additional design cases.

Safety integration is costed as one coherent cell with certified person detection, speed limiting and **Category1 stops**.
