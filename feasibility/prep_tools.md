## Principle

"**Appliances do the precise cutting; the arm loads, unloads and pulls levers.**"
- README.md l.36 (Prep row, R07–R11).
- DECISIONS.md 2026-10-09 "Market-first per function": "prefer that the appliance does the precise work, while the arm loads, unloads and transfers" (l.97).
- DECISIONS.md 2026-10-10 R11: "appliances do the precise cutting; the arm only loads, pushes, operates a lever, or lays produce on a belt. Appliance internals are abstracted as contained, mass-conserving state changes" (l.118).
- R19_PREP_AUDIT §5: "Rule: appliances do the precise work; domestic products first; multi-function bases; 3D printing allowed."
- Storage rule (DECISIONS 2026-10-09): light tools are fetched from a drawer / push-to-open storage and put back; only heavy or very frequent appliances (the slicer) stay on the counter. Fetch limit 3.5 kg.

## Summary
1. Principle (README l.36, DECISIONS 2026-10-09/10-10): bought appliances make the precise cut; the arm only loads chutes, places food, pushes/pulls levers, and fetches light tools from storage and puts them back.
2. Two cutters are proven in sim: the counter 卓晋 3-in-1 slicer (carrot chute drop, chain SD 7,431 states, ~28 s) and the fetched AY-403 lever base (tomato wedges, chain WD 5,992 states, ~22 s, ~5 N grip, ~100 N press).
3. The peeler is checked only as an abstract cradle; the real product (傲宏 85 W) is still open.
4. Mandoline, Breville food processor, standalone wedger and guillotine were tried or planned in R07–R10 and dropped in favour of the slicer + lever base.
5. R19_PREP_AUDIT: both checked chains are in an outdated layout (slicer spot now the plating area, peeler hits the spatula), and meat slicer, bowl chopper and egg cracker are proposed but missing; cutting forces (tomato wedge, greens bundles) are unsourced.
