# Feasibility gaps: electrical, power and wiring

Reviewed repo: `home-cooking-robot` (snapshot 2026-10-12, R19 notes 10-13). Read-only review, 2026-10-13. Line numbers refer to that snapshot.

## Summary
1. **The repo has no power budget for the current design.** The only ledgers are R09's 924 W "moving cart" figure (`sim/r10/claude/hardware.py`, `posts/r09/integrator.md` l.100–102) and `power_addendum.md`, which is for the R02 **rail** concept in **China at 220 V**. A rough estimate for a meal-A overlap (wok, zone 2, fryer, rice cooker, stew pot and robot) gives **about 9–10 kW, or 40–45 A**. That is above a 20 A kitchen socket circuit and about equal to an HDB 40 A main switch. The kitchen has 2 outlets (`inputs/user_kitchen/estimate.md`).
2. **The tether has not been designed since the controller moved off the cart in R10.** The only tether spec is a 230 V spring reel (3G1.5) chosen in R09, and the R09 reviewer marked it **BLOCKING** because its manual forbids uncoiling while powered. Nobody has closed that finding. An off-board UR controller means the tether must carry the UR arm cable, 48 V for the drives, Ethernet and the safety loop. None of this is specified.
3. **The heat sources have no independent, robot-controlled cut-off.** The 3.5 kW cooker is set by an open-loop servo on its knob. The stove guard cuts only the 20 A circuit. The E-stop is a line item with no defined scope. Zone 2, the fryer, the stew pot and the kettle sit on smart plugs or cloud MIoT with no safety role.
4. **Power loss or E-stop while the arm holds a hot wok has not been analysed.** The repo's own notes say the UR tool power drops to 0 V on E-stop (`research/hand_options.md` §2). The wok is then held only by the PGC "self-locking" flag (`hardware.py`, unverified), and nobody has checked what happens on recovery.
5. **Singapore compliance is missing.** Induction "hot plate/wok" units, rice cookers, slow cookers, fryers, kettles, plugs, adaptors and portable cable reels are all CPS Controlled Goods (SAFETY Mark). The bill of materials is China-market (220 V, GB plugs) or German (Schuko reel). The new 20 A point and any track or fixed wiring need a Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW). There is no single-line diagram, no wiring diagram and no EMC review.

## Top risks

**1. The kitchen cannot run the planned meal from its existing supply (#1–#5).** The repo states only that the wok needs a new 20 A point. Every other heater (zone 2 ≈ 2.1 kW, fryer 1.6–2.3 kW, rice cooker, stew pot, kettle) plus the robot (≈ 0.6–1 kW with the controller and PC) is assumed to plug in somewhere, on a kitchen that has two outlets. Meal A in `meal16.py` runs the wok, a 30-minute steam, a 35-minute rice cook and the fryer together. That is about 9–10 kW, or 40–45 A. This is my estimate from typical nameplates, since the repo gives no watts for most of these appliances. A typical SG kitchen socket circuit is 20 A. An HDB main switch is 40 A or 60 A, and the flat's aircon and water heater also share it. The fix is cheap and can be done on a laptop: add W per resource to `meal16.py`, enforce a cap per circuit, and see how much load shedding costs in meal time. Then do a site survey of the DB and get an LEW quote for 2 circuits rather than one. Without this the study could be designing a meal the flat cannot power.

**2. The tether is undefined, and the one reel on record was blocked (#6–#8).** R10 removed the 13.6 kg on-board controller to save weight. The tether, though, is still R09's 230 V spring reel (3G1.5, 1,000 W coiled), which the R09 reviewer marked BLOCKING because its manual forbids coiling or uncoiling while powered. With the controller off-board, the moving cable must carry the UR's proprietary arm cable as well as 48 V drive power, PE, Ethernet and a 2-channel safety loop. The cart must also follow the L corner to the sink leg and move 0.8 m with a hot wok in hand. The UR arm cable's length and flex rating for continuous motion are unknown. If it can't be routed through a chain, the controller has to go back on the cart, along with the mass the user wanted gone. This needs a conductor list and a cable-path drawing, then a cycle test on a bench.

**3. No reliable way to turn the heat off (#12–#15, #25).** The 3.5 kW cooker's only control is an open-loop servo on a knob nobody has examined. A knob-set cooker usually resumes at its setting after a power cut. The stove guard cuts only the cooker circuit, and only on rate of rise. Zone 2, the fryer, the stew pot and the kettle are on cloud MIoT or smart plugs with no safety role, and the E-stop has no defined scope. An unattended 250 °C wok with 30–40 g of oil is exactly the home-fire scenario the repo cites (318 of 1,051 SG fires in 2025). Design a contactor per heat circuit, driven by the E-stop, a PC watchdog, presence sensing and the thermal camera, with current-sensed confirmation and a deliberate re-arm. Then bench-test two candidate cookers for knob type and power-cut behaviour.

**4. A hot wok in the gripper when power or the E-stop drops (#16).** The repo notes that UR tool power drops to 0 V on E-stop. The wok (2.95 kg, food included) then hangs on a "self_locking=True" flag that was never checked against DH documentation, possibly tilted over a plate or out over the counter while the cart moves. Recovery is unstudied: DH grippers commonly run an initialisation stroke when power returns (to verify), and releasing the UR brakes by hand next to a 200 °C wok is hazardous. This needs a phase-by-phase FMEA, a cheap power-cut test with a 3 kg dummy, and a decision on a small UPS so the arm can finish a controlled SS1 stop and put the wok down.

**5. Compliance and plug mismatch (#21–#23).** In Singapore, induction "hot plate/wok" appliances, rice cookers, slow cookers, deep fryers, kettles, 3-pin plugs, adaptors, multi-way adaptors and portable cable reels are CPS Controlled Goods that need the SAFETY Mark. The BOM is China-market 220 V kit with GB plugs, a Schuko reel and Chinese power tracks, and a 3.5 kW Chinese unit ships with a 16 A GB plug that has no BS 1363 counterpart. All fixed wiring (cooker point, track, cabinet feed, contactor box) is LEW work under SS 638. None of this is in the repo, which researched these rules only for gas. A one-page compliance and plug matrix, plus a preference for SG-registered models, removes most of the risk at little cost. Whether a bolt-on knob servo is acceptable on a registered appliance should be confirmed.

Sources checked for SG facts (not in the repo): [CPSO list of controlled goods](https://www.consumerproductsafety.gov.sg/suppliers/cpsr/list-of-controlled-goods/); [HDB main-switch ratings, Home & Decor](https://www.homeanddecor.com.sg/how-to/hdb-electricity-loading) (40 A vs 60 A, sources disagree: verify on site); [Megafurniture on 16/20 A induction circuits](https://megafurniture.sg/blogs/articles/best-induction-hob-singapore-cost-guide) (vendor source).
