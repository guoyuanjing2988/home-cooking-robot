# Feasibility gaps: safety, hygiene and regulation

Reviewed: `home-cooking-robot` snapshot of 2026-10-12/13 (README.md, DECISIONS.md, sim/r10/claude/README_R10.md R10–R19, R19_MOTION_LIBRARY.md, research/r15_cooking_market.md, r14_storage_market.md, r19_system_cost.md, r19_arm_hand_cost.md, r19_seasoning_sizing.md, finger_design_inputs.md, finger_change_and_food_eoat.md, hand_options.md). Read-only review on 2026-10-13. I added nothing to the repo.

## Summary
1. **No hazard analysis exists.** There is no ISO 12100 / HARA / FMEA anywhere in the repo. The "home safety tier" (DECISIONS 2026-10-07) was written in R01 for a rail-mounted, prep-only robot whose main hazard was blades. It was never re-baselined after the scope moved to hot cooking (10-11) on a mobile cart (10-09).
2. **Hot food and oil around people are designed in but not analysed.** Power and force limiting (PFL) protects against impact, not against a hot payload. The user must reach into the workspace by design (clearing plates beside the wok, loading the rice cooker). Children, elderly people and pets are never mentioned.
3. **The fire protection is a product list, not a design.** The 250 °C limit is software reading a thermal camera. Nothing says the E-stop or a fault cuts the cookers. The chosen FireStop canister needs 69–89 cm above the burner, but the user's hood is only 0.40 m above the cooktop. There is no policy on unattended cooking.
4. **The wok hold has thin margins and an unknown power-loss behaviour.** The jaws run at 92 % of their moment limit. UR tool power drops to 0 V on E-stop, and whether the PGC then keeps its grip is undocumented. The 3D-printed PETG fingers (Tg 80 °C) grip a steel handle on a 200–250 °C wok, and the handle temperature was never estimated.
5. **Food safety is reduced to one rule: raw meat staged ≤ 1 h.** There is no cleaning procedure for the robot or fixtures, no control of raw-to-ready cross-contamination through shared fingers or the sink, and no check that meat is cooked through. Printed food-contact parts are porous. The Singapore regulatory items (CPS(SR)R / SAFETY Mark, the LEW-installed 20 A point, HDB rules, insurance) are open or absent.

Status key: **COVERED** = designed and evidenced in the repo; **PARTLY** = named, or partly designed, but not analysed or verified; **MISSING** = not in the repo. Effort: S ≤ 2 days, M ≤ 2 weeks, L > 2 weeks or needs an outside party.

## Top risks

**1. The safety case is from a different robot.** Everything that counts as "safety" in the repo goes back to the 2026-10-07 decision. That decision was written for rail-mounted arms doing washing and cutting, and it names blades as the main hazard. Since then the system has become a mobile 65 kg cart carrying a 200–250 °C wok and hot oil, running heaters on its own, in a home with possible children and pets. No document re-derives the hazards. So the ¥10–20k "home tier" (presence sensor, E-stop, RCD, brakes) is a budget, not the result of an analysis. The repo already contains the best possible task list for closing this: the R19 movement library, with named sets, pre/post-conditions and swept volumes. A task-based ISO 12100 risk assessment over those sets is a laptop job of about a week. Its output should drive items 4, 11–12, 15, 18 and 20.

**2. Hot loads near people, where PFL does not help.** PFL and ISO/TS 15066 limit the contact force on a body. They do not limit what happens when a robot holding 1.15 kg of food at 200 °C (plus oil) brushes a person or makes a protective stop. The design also requires the person to enter the zone: the user clears plates from two plating spots right next to the wok's tip path, loads the rice cooker, and fills the kettle and jug. The only runtime rule is "person in reach → pause, cobot holds". That leaves a hot wok held in the air next to the person, maybe a child at head height. On top of that, the hot wok is rinsed under a cold running tap within about 50 s of the heat going off (flash steam and spatter), and in the real L-shaped kitchen the sink is on the other leg, so the hot-carry route is not even modelled. Closing this needs three things: an explicit "hot-carry" class of movements, a person-free zone during those movements verified by a safety-rated sensor (which the 10-07 exception clause allows), and a defined hand-off protocol. The slosh-under-stop simulation and the zone layout can be done on a laptop. Stop distances and splash behaviour need bench tests.

**3. Fire: software limit, no hardware heat cut, suppression that does not fit, no supervision rule.** Unattended cooking caused 30 % of Singapore home fires in 2025, and the repo cites this figure itself. Yet the design leaves the cookers running "unattended" while the arm works elsewhere. The 250 °C limit lives in application code reading a thermal camera whose emissivity errors the repo documents. The knob is moved by a bolt-on servo, and the "≈280 °C dry-burn cut-off" is a typical value, not a property of the chosen unit. Nothing de-energises the cookers on an E-stop, a crash or a lost heartbeat. The chosen canister needs 69–89 cm above the burner, but the hood is 40 cm above the cooktop, and the canister also needs a flame and shallow oil. Cloud-connected (MIoT) heaters add a remote-ignition path. Recommended closure: a fail-off contactor plus an independent contact high-limit on every heat source, wired to the E-stop and a watchdog; local-only control; a suppression product re-selected for a 40 cm hood; and a written user decision on supervision (an adult at home, periodic acknowledgement). The design part is laptop work. The contactor needs a LEW.

**4. Wok retention under faults.** The wok is held at 6.47 of 7 N·m jaw moment, at DH's recommended load limit, by 3D-printed PETG/TPU fingers. Three things are unknown: whether the PGC keeps its grip when UR tool power drops to 0 V on an E-stop (the repo's own UR manual extract says it does drop), how hot the steel grip block gets at the end of a 0.235 m handle on a 250 °C wok (PETG softens at about 80 °C), and whether the arm brakes hold (marked "ND for all" in R19). A dropped hot wok onto feet or the floor is a severe burn scenario. The palm-key pin coupling proposed in r19_arm_hand_cost §3.2 is the right direction: it takes the moment off the jaws. It should be adopted together with a mechanical latch and an insulating block, and confirmed by a protective-stop dynamics re-run (laptop), a thermocouple test and a water-filled drop test (hardware).

**5. Food hygiene has one rule and no procedure.** The only food-safety design item is "raw meat ≤ 1 h out" plus a cumulative-time rule in research. The following do not exist: a cleaning SOP for the arm, fingers, trays, drum, pump lines, slicer and cutter (or a count of the user minutes it costs); a separation of raw protein from produce on the same printed fingers, the shared sink and the spatula pad; any core-temperature check for meat judged done by camera; and any ambient-time budget once the 21–40 min of robot prep is added to meals of 38–76 min at Singapore's ~30 °C. Porous FDM pads with spring pockets cannot be sanitised: PETG's Tg of 80 °C rules out hot-water sanitising. Most of this can be designed on a laptop in days (SOP, exposure timeline from `meal16.py`, a dedicated raw-protein tool, lidded and chilled trays). Proving it needs swab (ATP) tests and probe-thermometer cooks with real food.
