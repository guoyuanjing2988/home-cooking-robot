# Gaps review: cooking process, operations, user workload and value

Scope: the home-cooking-robot repo snapshot (`home-cooking-robot/`, R19, 2026-10-13), read only. Goal under review: one UR5e on a tethered cart cooks and plates the reference cook's 四菜一汤 (meals A/B/C, `research/homestyle_meals.md`) in the user's L-shaped Singapore kitchen. The user serves and clears.
Tags: **[repo]** = number taken from a repo file; **[calc]** = my arithmetic on repo numbers; **[est]** = my estimate, not in the repo; **[ext]** = public figure from outside the repo, not checked here.

## Summary (5 lines)
1. **No dish of the 15 is covered end to end.** Every dish needs at least one prep operation the R19 audit marks [missing] (mince, eggs, meat slicing, florets, luffa/bamboo peel, marinate, arrange). The only checked prep chains (carrot, tomato) are in an old layout and no longer valid there.
2. **Reliability is not modelled at all.** A meal is about 110–160 arm actions and about 190–310 grasp/release events [calc]. At 99.5 % per action, a meal succeeds 29–47 % of the time. A 90 % meal success needs about 99.93–99.96 % per action (1 failure in about 1,400–2,400). There is no failure catalogue or recovery design.
3. **The user's workload is not counted anywhere, and the net time saved is unproven.** Loading trays, water, rice, clearing plates, cleaning about 20 robot-specific items (no cleaning design exists), restocking and rescues come to about 45–90 min per meal [est]. A human cooking the same meal with cleanup takes about 75–120 min [est; no baseline was measured]. The saving could be anywhere from about −15 to +75 min.
4. **Real-food physics is untested.** Stirring is rigid dice only (shreds, slices and leaves are "not credible"), plating has no sauce, there is no residue/scrub model, cutting forces exceed the arm's ~100 N for some items, and doses are mostly "1 spoon / a little" (ND). Taste tuning (15 dishes × several cooks) is not planned.
5. **Value.** With the cheap arm the system is about ¥147–195k (≈S$27–35k), or ≈S$31–60k with the UR5e [calc from r19_system_cost], before labour, electrician and fire items. That equals about 1.5–4.5 years of a Singapore domestic helper [ext], who also does every other chore. The cheapest next step is a **no-robot "one-hand, appliance-only" cooking trial** in the real kitchen.

## Top risks

**1. Meal-level reliability (rows 20–22).** Repo counts give about 110–160 arm actions and about 190–310 grasp/release events per meal (prep instances A 106 / B 67 / C 44, ≈36 cooking actions and ≈99 cooking grasps per meal, 12–27 seasoning moves, 2–3 fridge trips). With independent steps, meal success is p^n: 0.995^150 = 47 %, 0.995^250 = 29 %, 0.99^150 = 22 %, 0.999^250 = 78 %. A 90 % meal success needs ≥99.93 % per action at n = 150, and ≥99.96 % at n = 250 (1 failure in about 1,400–2,400). Research-grade food manipulation is far below this, and the checked paths keep only 1–2 mm reserves against fixtures that the user moves daily. Nothing in the repo models failure rates, detection or recovery. The arm stalling over a hot wok also makes "failure" a safety issue. Close it with a reliability allocation per motion family, an FMEA with recoveries, and a single-dish real-arm campaign that measures per-step success (stage 3 below) before more sim breadth.

**2. Prep coverage (rows 1, 4–6).** The user made prep a robot job (DECISIONS 10-13), but the only checked prep is two single-piece chains in a layout that no longer exists. The mince, egg, meat, floret, peel, marinate and arrange families are all [missing], and the prep estimate of 21–40 arm-min per meal is a stated lower bound. Every dish depends on at least one missing op, so the 39–76 min meal times in the README do not yet describe a meal that the hardware can make. Close it with the dish × step matrix, the purchase-form decisions, and the prep-in-scheduler run with sink/counter resources.

**3. Net value to the user (rows 16, 17, 24).** The design moves work to the user (loading trays one item per slot, water, rice, plate clearing on call, restocking) and creates new work: washing about 20 robot-specific parts (slicer, peeler, chopper, meat slicer, cups/drum, baskets, trays, pads, guards, nozzles) for which no cleaning design exists. My ledger gives 45–90 min of user time per meal against an unmeasured human baseline of perhaps 75–120 min, so the saving may be small or negative. On cost, ≈S$27–35k (FR5 path) to ≈S$31–60k (UR5e) up front plus maintenance compares with ≈S$13–18k a year for a helper [ext: S$800–1,200 wage plus S$300 levy (S$60 concessionary)], who also cleans and shops. A ¥500–4,000 auto-cooker plus the user is the low-cost baseline. The value case should be written down with these numbers and a go/no-go threshold (for example, ≥30 min saved per meal at ≥90 % meal success).

**4. Real-food process physics and taste (rows 8–13).** Every cooking result comes from rigid pieces: shreds, slices and leaves are explicitly "not credible", the stir period must slow to ~3.6 s, plating loses 10–19 % without sauce, residue and scrubbing are unmodelled, some cutting forces exceed the arm's ~100 N, and the reference cook's doses are mostly unstated. Even a perfect robot then needs a taste-tuning campaign of about 45–75 real cooks. These are cheap to test by hand and should come before any further sim refinement.

**5. The real kitchen (rows 25–26).** The sim is a straight 2.9 m run with the sink 0.9 m from the wok. The real kitchen is an L with the sink on the other leg, the counter clearance under the cabinets is only 0.50–0.55 m (estimated), and the aisle, fridge position, parking spot and electrical capacity are unknown. Every checked path, the hub roadmap and the meal times depend on this layout. One hour with a tape measure retires the largest geometric unknown. The R19 swept-volume screen then shows which stored motions survive.

## Staged path (cheapest risk retirement first)
- **Stage 0, laptop (1 week):** coverage matrix (#1–2), reliability budget and FMEA (#20–21), user ledger and value case (#16, #24), tray/cold-box sizing (#6–7), electrical table (#26).
- **Stage 1, real, no robot (~S$300–700, 2 weekends) — the key step:** measure the kitchen (#25). Then the user cooks meals B and C under the robot's rules: no knife (only the chosen slicer, lever cutter, chopper and egg cracker), one hand plus tools, the 3.6 s spatula stroke without tossing, tip-plating into a mock guarded stand, a rinse-only wok, every dose weighed, the wok camera recording, and a stopwatch on every user task and on all cleaning. This retires rows 3, 8–17 and 15 in a few days, with no robot bought.
- **Stage 2, bench (~S$100–300):** tilt-friction test, cutting forces, one peristaltic channel with loss-in-weight dosing of 蚝油, brine and slurry.
- **Stage 3, one cheap arm on a fixed table (FR5 ≈¥24k + PGC):** one wok dish (清炒荷兰豆, then 番茄炒蛋) 30–50 times. Measure per-step success, interventions and recovery against the stage-0 budget. Buy the cart, seasoning station and prep set only if this gate passes.
- **Stage 4:** cart plus a full meal in the measured kitchen.
