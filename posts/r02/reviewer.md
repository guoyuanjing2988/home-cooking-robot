2. **BLOCKING — Arm torque and spring-brake eligibility still repeat R01's unresolved assumptions.** The xArm6 reference `[64,64,32,32,12,12]` Nm remains rated motor torque times ideal 100:1 gearing, not published continuous/rated output-joint capacity. The [manufacturer joint table](https://docs.supportarticle.ufactory.cc/support_articles/hardware/ufactory-joint-parameters.html) distinguishes motor generations, gearing and overload; it does not validate this output-duty limit. The preserved older model mass is 12.05164 kg rather than the current 12.5 kg specification; its wider J2/J3 ranges also need matching. Any-direction mounting is supported by [xArm specifications](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/8.technical_specifications.html); that does not close the working wrench or brake proof. Do not substitute peak or URDF effort ratings to obtain a pass.

3. **BLOCKING — Several new process-force bounds are unqualified proxies.** All 17 force models have an identifiable source or explicit equation, and the arithmetic is reproducible. That is good traceability, but it is insufficient physical validation for tomato wedging, bok choy cutting, eye/core scooping and potato peeling. Probe compression is not blade cutting; carrot transverse resistance is not a demonstrated upper bound for fibrous greens or an eye-removal spoon. A factor of two cannot establish that relationship. No evidence of tuning forces to pass was found, and the authors disclose the proxies. Treat these values as structural placeholders until blade geometry, speed, food maturity and force/displacement measurements establish a defensible bound plus margin. The six literature spot-checks below distinguish sound sizing from unsupported transfer. A 1,000 N actuator/frame rating is capacity, not evidence of the food force or acceptable damage.

4. **HIGH — Finished-product requirements are specified, but important loops and states are not executable.** The new recipe retains all four products and the old P01–P16 purposes; nothing justifies dropping washing, eyes, terminal slices, starch rinses, greens creases or separate cooking bowls. Bowl installation/swap, scrap diversion, the last two 5 mm root slices and repeated potato decant/drain operations need explicit states and routes. Some loops are stated in prose, so they are not silent recipe deletions, but a checked representative stroke does not verify them. Q16's ten-minute soak is omitted from the timing ledger. Inspect wash quality and cut/yield outcomes physically; pose acceptance cannot certify them. The same-X crossover is clear, but the complete travel corridor is not. 8. **HIGH — Home safety must be implemented and tested, without importing an industrial cell.** Apply DECISIONS.md: collaborative/PFL and collision functions, low tool/rail speeds, inexpensive presence sensing, an E-stop, monitored stop/brake interfaces and guarded interlocked cutting. Do not demand scanners, light curtains or a safety PLC as defaults. The xArm manual assigns integration responsibility and warns against people in the work area; low speed and a cobot label do not prove safe overhead contact or pinch forces. Use a guard interlock that prevents reachable powered blades, verifies blade stop/park before access, catches a gravity-driven press and prevents automatic restart after door, power or network recovery. Presence sensors are secondary protection for contact/pinch exposure, not the sole blade safeguard. The [OEM stop table](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/7.production_information.html) assigns Category1 to EI/emergency stop and Category2 to SI/safeguard stop. Six distinct new process-force assumptions were checked against primary literature. Values below are design-margin values, not measurements of the delivered fixtures.

These sources support the limitations as well as the arithmetic.

Arms still carry vessels/cassettes and encounter drawer, clamp, peeling, washing and core-tool reactions. Qualify the fixed frame/feet, indexing and blade jam loads separately. A separate [primary high-speed cutting study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0260877404000986) investigated carrot cutting at roughly 0.175–4.04 m/s with different blade geometry. It supports rejecting a universal sub-mm/s restriction; it does not transfer its forces to this press. Household motor power is not a direct calculation of peak food force.

**Defensible speed decision:** for roots, investigate a guarded OEM rotary slicer at its rated **375 rpm** with a genuine 5 mm slicing disc, rather than retain the custom slow press as the default. Assuming one working edge per revolution gives 6.25 cuts/s and a theoretical 31.25 mm/s axial product feed at 5 mm per cut. For small-batch planning, derate to **one slice/s, or 5 mm/s average axial feed**: about 50 s for the reference 32 carrot and 18 potato cuts, plus loading and setup. This is product feed, not blade plunge speed. Exact shape, terminal yield, feeding force and disc results still need trials. A compact household appliance must establish the actual 5 mm setting; no published rpm is invented for it. Use a separate supported eight-sector tomato tool and greens length/width guide, because a generic slicer/dicer cannot produce every required shape.

For the current custom press, **20 mm/s is only an instrumented test target**, not a validated operating recommendation at the old forces. A 5 mm lead needs 240 rpm; ideal screw demand at 1,000 N and 85% efficiency is 0.936 Nm and mechanical power 20 W. The 150 W supply allowance is plausible in magnitude but does not prove torque at speed, jam stopping or food quality. At 5/10/20 mm/s, retaining the original handling allowances and adding the reference soak gives serial sensitivity **50.6/46.1/43.9 min** respectively; all require new force-speed trials.

**Reference meal estimate:** one 0.12 kg carrot, 0.20 kg potato, 0.10 kg bok choy and 0.15 kg tomato; 0.57 kg total. Submitted 40 mm/min cutting consumes 75.4 min; serial time is 101.7 min and the listed resource schedule 95.0 min. Q16 allocates 90 s while the tools recipe retains ten minutes soaking. Adding that wait at the existing serial point gives **111.7 min serial / 105.0 min scheduled**. The tools' 0.5 mm/s proposal is slower still. Soaking is a reference quality process, not a universal ten-minute acceptance rule: shortening it requires equivalent wash evidence.

For the rotary-disc alternative, retain 26.35 min of non-cut handling, 50 s root slicing, roughly two minutes other guarded cuts/setup, ten-minute soak and the existing six minutes inspection/cleanup embedded in handling. That yields **39.2 min arithmetic serial**, budget **39–43 min**. Start greens soaking in a separate removable clean tub before root peeling/coring, avoiding occupation of the active sink, and overlap most of the wait: **30–35 min** is a defensible planning target. Re-layout, tool changes and trajectories remain unverified; there is no validated total prep time. The commercial 7.7 s capacity-equivalent for 0.32 kg roots is not a realistic autonomous meal-time claim. [Timing assumptions and calculations](../../sim/r02/reviewer/results/throughput.json).

## Next steps

1. **Repair feasibility before architecture expansion.** Resolve drawer/cassette/support approaches, receiving heights, acquisition jaw geometry and full tool/cradle/bowl states. Run all 27 logical phases with frozen carriage axes and actual assemblies; connect admitted poses into collision-checked motions. Include continuous empty passing, parked tools, open doors, chain sweep and ≥10 mm infrastructure clearance under load/tolerance. Diagnose questionable mesh/wall distances without changing gates.
2. **Qualify hardware from public evidence.** Select a matched buyable principal arm with eligible output-torque data, ≥3 kg task payload plus EOAT and documented spring-applied gravity-axis brakes. Close custom gripper/keys or model the real commercial one. Size portal/anchors and rack brake including stop loads. Preserve the exact checker constants and admitted-versus-diagnostic distinction.
3. **Build isolated process evidence first.** Instrument carrot/potato slicing and peeling, bok choy trim/split/section cuts, tomato coring/eight-wedge cut, drawers and washing pads across ripeness/size/sharpness variation. Measure force/displacement at proposed speeds, derive a bound with margin and verify every finished-product criterion, terminal slice and ≥98% bowl yield. Prefer a guarded commodity root slicer with direct bowl output; investigate a compact location/device rather than forcing a 595 mm commercial appliance below 500 mm cabinets.
4. **Implement the home safety and hygiene assembly.** Match track SKU/IP/current and converter/RCD data; keep stationary mains outlets dry and arm leads in separate chains. Test guard stop-before-open, press retention, E-stop Category1, power loss, data loss, manual reset, rail/gravity brakes and presence response. Demonstrate safe cleaning and food-contact declarations for the complete path. Fit removable covers in the same collision model.
5. **Reconcile time and cost after evidence.** Retain soaking, drain/rinse cycles, inspection and cleaning in the resource schedule. Compare two full arms with one main plus small helper under identical rules, public costs and the same completed meal. Only a fully passed workflow and qualified home-safety assembly justify proceeding to an integrated prototype.
