## Findings

1. **BLOCKING — Torque eligibility fails the binding rule.** `model.py` puts `[64,64,32,32,12,12]` Nm into fields named `continuous_Nm`. They are motor rated torque multiplied by 100:1 gearing, with ideal transmission efficiency, rather than published continuous/rated joint-output limits. UR5e maximum values, FR3 saturation bounds and AUBO URDF effort are also ineligible. The concept explicitly marks `binding_rated_admission=false`; that disclosure is appropriate, but does not cure the missing requirement. An assumed 85% efficiency sensitivity is not a thermal or output-rating certificate. Keep these runs as diagnostics and obtain eligible public manufacturer data for a matched model before binding admission. The [manufacturer joint table](https://docs.supportarticle.ufactory.cc/support_articles/hardware/ufactory-joint-parameters.html) separates rated motor values, gearing, overload and hardware generations; it does not establish the required output duty rating. The market torque ledger reaches the same conclusion.

2. **HIGH — Six missing phases prevent a complete preparation workflow.** P01 ingredient acquisition, P12 greens cutting, P13 tomato wedging, P14 collection/transfer and P15/P16 tool pickup/return are unresolved. Passing washing and mandoline strokes does not deliver an autonomous prep cycle. The supplied cooking research also requires surface cleaning, peel/eye removal, 5±1 mm roots, 40±10 mm greens, eight tomato wedges and edible yield; endpoint checks do not measure these outcomes. Retain those as later process acceptance work. Do not import the older cooking checker's different friction or neighbourhood rules into this binding T08 screen.

3. **HIGH — Source-model fidelity is good, but hardware revision closure is missing.** Independent comparison confirms xArm6 link transforms, axes, masses, COMs and inertias against the pinned older official files. The repository commits match. However, the imported arm totals **12.05164 kg**, versus official pages giving **12.2 or 12.5 kg**. The legacy xacro uses approximately J2 −118…120° and J3 −225…11°; the main specification page gives −117…116° and −219…10°. Four admitted pose records exceed the narrower ranges: P03 seed85 J2 −117.972°, P04 seeds26/64 J3 −224.667°/−225.001°, and P07 seed78 J3 −222.795°. Filtering those witnesses leaves all ten conditional phases represented; this is not evidence that the broader legacy range is fabricated. The [official legacy comparison](https://docs.supportarticle.ufactory.cc/support_articles/hardware/the-difference-between-ufactory-xarm5-xarm6-and-xarm7.html) supports the broader ranges, while the [main specifications](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/8.technical_specifications.html) differ. Match the buyable revision, mass distribution, firmware limits and torque table. xArm6's any-direction mounting is supported. FR3 angled/inverted sweep entries must remain excluded under the market's cited upright-only manual and insufficient payload.

4. **HIGH — Required 3 kg capacity is unproved, even using the optimistic torque reference.** An independent added-capacity diagnostic at P06 seed52/start, with 3 kg held mass and the unchanged nominal force `[-40,+10,+20]` N, requires **12.026 Nm at J5**, ratio **1.00213** against the ideal 12 Nm reference. The same pose at the original diagnostic check force `[-60,+10,+20]` N reproduces **17.680 Nm**, ratio **1.47330**. Check forces remain diagnostic in the original admission logic; this review does not turn them into a new phase gate. Other nominal capacity witnesses remain available, so this is not a proof that every configuration fails. Also close the mass boundary: 3 kg task payload must be carried **plus** gripper, adapters and retained tool mass; replacing a combined food/tool `held_kg` with 3.0 kg is not that complete ledger. The 1.2 kg gripper gives 4.2 kg before further carried tooling. A [dated manufacturer comparison](https://assets.zyrosite.com/m6Lb1n8yxOiXQr53/ufactory-product-list-dOqDEyxRogfNgOKP.pdf) qualifies xArm6's 5 kg payload at half reach. Nominal payload alone does not qualify this working wrench and TCP offset.

5. **HIGH — The custom gripper is an unqualified hardware assumption.** Its 1.2 kg mass, 90 mm opening, 150 N opposing normal force and 150 N/15 Nm keyed capacities are preserved reference/design assumptions. They are not a rated commercial product or a proven custom assembly. The native xArm gripper has only 30 N published maximum force and cannot silently substitute for it. Demonstrate actuator retention, jaw guidance, keyed force/moment paths, finger stiffness and food-compatible removable pads at the stated mass. The market's DH examples have moment ratings below 15 Nm; selecting them by advertised grip force alone is insufficient. Preserve μ=0.5 and SF=1.5 during that work.

6. **HIGH — Cable-carrier geometry understates the physical return bend.** The moving loop is only **200×25×60 mm**. The [manufacturer cable data](https://docs.supportarticle.ufactory.cc/support_articles/hardware/bend-radius-for-communication-and-power-cable.html) specifies power cable diameter 10.4 mm and radius 78 mm; a longitudinal U-turn needs roughly **166.4 mm** outer transverse span before chain walls and clearance. The modeled transverse diagonal is only 65 mm. A real return loop therefore extends outside the proxy or needs a different routing architecture. Model the full changing chain shape, fixed ends, cable separation, bending radius and connectors throughout all carriage travel, then recheck cabinets, faucet and outlets. This is a geometry shortfall, not a numerical tolerance change.

7. **HIGH — Rail and anchors are load inputs, not a sized installation.** The 30×70 mm beam envelope, 24 kg mass and four supports at x=−1.50, −0.75, 0 and +0.55 m do not specify material, section, guide-block capacity or anchor substrate. The right support leaves **1.75 m** of beam overhang and 1.57 m to a parked carriage. Concept statics report 240 N per carriage, 103.6 Nm interface moment and **577.1 Nm** outer-support envelope; these include diagnostic poses and are not one anchor's reaction. An illustrative steel RHS30×70×3 mm under just 240 N at 1.57 m deflects about **4.65 mm**, before arm moment, beam weight, torsion or stop dynamics. That is an illustration, not a selected section or new acceptance gate. The [manufacturer installation instructions](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/2.hardware_installation.html) require a stiff mount and multiples of base torsion and arm weight; static phase loads alone do not satisfy them. Solve beam/support reactions, torsional stiffness, carriage bearing moments, rack/brake loads, clustered arms and stops. Kitchen cabinet panels cannot be the structural load path.

8. **BLOCKING — Spring-applied joint and rail brake requirements remain unverified.** The [safety manual](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/1.safety.html) describes internal joint brakes and power-outage holding, but does not establish each axis's spring-applied construction, holding torque or engagement timing. Side/angled mounting can gravity-load axes differently from upright mounting. Freezing a rail joint in IK is not a brake test. Specify a real de-energize-to-apply brake on each drive, including rating at rack output and any emergency-work limits, and match each gravity-loaded arm axis to public brake evidence. Check sudden mains loss, retained-tool/gripper loss, brake delay and restart. A powered Category1 trajectory cannot be promised after instantaneous loss of all power without a demonstrated existing drive-energy capability. No battery is proposed.

9. **BLOCKING — Human detection and Category1 stop integration are not closed.** Proposed 0.10 m/s carriage and 0.15 m/s tool limits are starting settings, not established safe separation. A kitchen person can reach around fixtures or into a scanner-occluded region; knives and the mandoline require intrusion prevention regardless of cobot branding. The [manufacturer stop table](https://docs.xarm.ufactory.cc/7.production_information.html) assigns **Category1 to EI/emergency stop and Category2 to SI/safeguard stop**. A scanner on SI alone would not deliver the requested Category1 behavior. Provide a matched safety input/drive/brake architecture, monitored controlled stop, protective-field coverage, access/door interlocks, manual reset and measured stopping distance for both rail and arms. Ordinary cameras and simulation collision checks do not validate that chain.

10. **HIGH — Splash, grease and sanitation provisions are still allowances.** Washing places the arms and cables beside water, while the protected stove's geometry does not prevent grease aerosol from adjacent human cooking. The manufacturer environment guidance excludes oil mist and corrosive liquid; installation guidance warns against direct liquid contact. The market's drainable covers, wipers and remote controllers are sensible but are not in the complete collision/mass assembly. Specify removable washable EOAT, drip paths, sealed connectors, covered lubricated guides, compatible cleaning agents, protected electrical enclosures and contamination-separated dirty/clean processing. Verify that covers remain clear of the faucet, outlets and doors and do not trap food or obstruct service. A cleanroom rating does not establish washdown or food-contact suitability.

11. **MEDIUM — Cost evidence is candid, but the architectures were not reconciled.** The market's CNY464,600/599,150 totals use Zu7 and **2.4 m** rail; the concept needs xArm6 and **4.6 m** to achieve its parking/reach result. The reviewed estimate substitutes an explicitly assumed **CNY77,000 per complete arm/controller**, increases rail and covers by length, and retains the other disclosed allowances. The dated official xArm6 EXW price is USD8,399; at the market's planning 7.00 rate that is CNY58,793 before unmatched inclusions/import costs. The inherited CNY76,900 tender reproduction could not be independently retrieved and is not represented as a verified current quotation. Safety sensors, commissioning and custom EOAT dominate the remaining budget; chain/support redesign may raise it. Comparing an installed custom cell with the old approximate CNY470k mobile-platform price does not establish savings. See the full [cost reconciliation](../../sim/r01/reviewer/audit/cost_reconciliation.json).

12. **MEDIUM — A third arm has no demonstrated value here; arm-family ranking is not fair performance evidence.** The two-arm final search admits ten conditional phases and the three-arm search nine. The submitted identical-pose replay loses two stroke witnesses when the third arm parks at the left end; it does not supply a permitted force-sharing benefit. Single-lane carriages cannot pass, and no complete relocation/scheduling path is supplied. The reviewed third-arm increment is **CNY136,850** with extra brakes, chains, inspection and occlusion. Keep two arms; reconsider a third only after a concrete parallel-work schedule or stabilising task has been defined and separately validated. UR5e/FR3/AUBO family rows mostly have two seeds versus 96 for the winner, and some are payload/mount-ineligible. The concept discloses this, but its ranking cannot establish that xArm6 intrinsically outperforms those arms. Moley's multi-axis platform and Samsung's demo are useful fixture/workspace precedents, not evidence of a buyable one-axis rail passing these tasks. [Moley A-AiR](https://www.moley.com/a-air-kitchen/), [Samsung's introduction](https://news.samsung.com/global/get-a-glimpse-of-the-next-generation-innovations-on-display-at-samsungs-technology-showcase).
